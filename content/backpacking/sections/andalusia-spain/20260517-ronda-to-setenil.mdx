After having met two amazing people from Norway (of all places), and the some bad coffee for breakfast, we were ready to start our hike to Setenil.

This was our first time ever hiking through mountains, so we were pretty excited to see the views from the mountains.

    After a kilometer or so, we were leaving Ronda behind and the nature started opening up. We were climbing up a pretty high mountain that we weren't used to. In the Netherlands the highest point we had to hike on the [Pieterpad](../../trails/20230707-pieterpad.mdx) was around 25 metres.

    After we climbed our first mountain, we didn't even realize there were more to come, the civilization seemed to come back and we were walking on a regular road next to these big villas in the hot sun. Luckily, the nature started peaking around the corner and we could finally walk through the shade again. Little did we know that this was the beginning of the second mountain were we climbed for 

    When walking on top of the mountain we could really see the nature and the surprisingly few houses that are in the area. When we were going down the mountain we crossed a river twice. The first time the water was so deep that we had to take off our shoes before crossing. It was a nice refreshment about halfway through the hike.

    After we crossed the water we saw a lot of plants that are also native to the Netherlands, and all of the sudden there was also a grapevine just growing through the bushes. As someone who only saw grapevines in backyards it was amazing to see them growing in the wild.

    In the picture you see the second river crossing and just how blue the skies were. Since this water crossing wasn't as wide as the first one, we were able to sort of walk/jump over it.

    When we finally got to Setenil we were feeling our feet pretty well, since this was one of the longest walks we've done in a while. In the hotel we stayed in, we took off our shoes and put on our slippers, which is a really good feeling after a long hike. Since we were in Setenil, we offcourse went to look at the famous street under a rock where there are restaurants and houses that are built into the rock.
    The only downside to this was, that our hotel was at the other side of Setenil, so we had to walk for about 20 minutes. Now this isn't long by far, but after a hike of around 6 hours it was pretty hard to put our minds into the correct head space.

    The picture you see here isn't the famous street you'll see in every picture, but I think this is a really nice view of the houses that are also built into the rock (and there aren't as much tourists on this picture, so that's a plus as well).
</ImageFloat>

We've been to the famous street as well and had a really good bun from the bakery, I don't remember the name, but is was something with Almond and caramel. So if you're ever in the area just go to the bakery and ask for a local pastry, you won't regret it!

Of course, we also had to eat something and why not try one of the restaurants under the rock? We both ordered the _Pajuna_ beef, and it is probably the best steak I've ever had. With the beef they also served patatas bravas, which I also really liked. So the entire meal was extremely good, the fact that we were tired and hungry also helped a lot.
